## L1 — a scale-free admission for the questions list: measured, not shipped

The gap: `QUESTIONS_GATE` admits the generated-questions BM25 list to the plain fusion when its best
score is at least 0.85 of the passage list's best, and those two scores are in two units — each
index normalises length against its own mean (30.6 tokens a passage document, 51.8 a question
document) and weights terms by its own vocabulary (10,762 terms against 21,839) — both from
[the design note](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-0.5.0-gaps-design.md). That is G8 (the
constant is a property of one store) and G12 (the comparison itself is between two units), and G13
is the table it would be judged on.

### The rule, as pre-registered

Copied from [the design note](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-0.5.0-gaps-design.md), not paraphrased:

> **Rule for stage A** — every clause, none re-read after the numbers:
>
> - (i) the recorded suite reproduces the four arms **exactly** on the fixture store:
>   `40/40 15/30 12/12` and `39/40 14/30 12/12` enriched, `40/40 9/30 12/12` and `39/40 7/30 12/12`
>   raw, p90 ≤ 230 in each;
> - (ii) the 400 held-out questions are not significantly worse in either arm against the shipped
>   binary's dumps (paired exact McNemar, α = 0.05, `bench/heldout.py compare`);
> - (iii) the developer suite (60) is not down in either arm;
> - (iv) on a second population of the same corpus — the fixture's questions cut to their first
>   five per node, zero tokens, deterministic — the new admission is not significantly worse than
>   the ratio on that store's own held-out set in either arm, and reads at least what the ratio
>   reads on the 82 there. This is the clause that says "scale-free" rather than "fitted again".

And the three forms, in this order and no other: **coverage** (a list's best over what the query
could attain in that index — the score a document of average length holding each query term once
would get, which BM25 makes the sum of the terms' idf), **peak** (a list's best over its own fifth
score), **z** (the best as a z-score over the list 20 deep). Each form has one constant `c`, and `c`
is the crossover on the 400 held-out questions in the `--no-dense` arm: the split of the sorted
statistic that puts the most questions on the side of the list that holds their answer in its top
five, ties to the lower value. The 82 and the 60 are opened only after `c` is fixed.

### What was measured, and what it is

`bench/admission.py` replays the plain fusion offline over `dump` records — it is not a changed
binary. Before any constant was derived, the replay had to reproduce the shipped binary's own
answers under `ratio 0.85` (`$G/t2-check.txt`):

```
t1-rec-dense.json: 82/82 replicated      t1-dev-dense.json: 60/60 replicated      t1-ho-dense.json: 400/400 replicated
t1-rec-lexical.json: 82/82 replicated    t1-dev-lexical.json: 60/60 replicated    t1-ho-lexical.json: 400/400 replicated
```

That check compares the seated id sequences, not the recorded scores or `via` values, so it proves
the replay seats what the binary seats and not full `Ask` fidelity. Scored under the shipped rule,
the replay reads the shipped numbers back (`$G/t2-score-ratio.txt`): recorded `40/40 15/30 12/12`
and `39/40 14/30 12/12`, developer totals 36 and 37 — the kind sums of the baseline lines above —
and held-out 103/400 and 109/400 with 0 gained and 0 lost in both arms.

Two limits on the scope, stated because clause (i) names four arms and this replay reads two. The
dumps are the fixture's, and the fixture is enriched: **the two raw arms were never replayed**. They
did not need to be — every form already failed on the enriched half — and the reason runs deeper
than that: a raw store builds no questions index at all (`Lexical::build`, read by `src/query.rs`'s
`lexical_lists`), so there is no questions list for any admission form to gate in the first place —
a raw arm cannot move under this lever by construction. Task 3, which would have confirmed a
surviving form live on all four arms, never ran. Clause (iv) was likewise never read: it is scored
only for a form that survives clause (i), and none did.

### Verdict, and why the rule was not re-read

All three forms failed clause (i); the verdict file's first line is `form=none`
(`$G/admission-verdict.txt`). `QUESTIONS_GATE = 0.85` is unchanged, `src/query.rs` was not touched,
and **there is no ADR-001 amendment** — the admission did not change. Stage B, which would have
admitted the code-questions list under a shipped form and its constant, needs a shipped form and was
not run; A4's `where` 2/9 at six held-out questions lost per arm (p = 0.031) remains the number to
beat there.

Clause (i) says "exactly". Coverage's only failure is one paraphrase *gained* in the lexical arm,
with both developer arms and both held-out arms up. Re-reading (i) as "holds every floor" now, with
the numbers in hand, is exactly the move ADR-001 exists to forbid, and Rule 3 of this project says
a lever that fails its rule is written down with its numbers and no constant is tuned after a suite
has been read. So coverage is recorded as measured and not shipped, at the cost of an improvement
left on the table for one release.

Nothing about it needs re-measuring to change that. The constant, the crossover, both recorded arms,
both developer arms and both held-out arms are above for each of the three forms. A future plan that
pre-registers "every floor held, developer suite not down, held-out not worse" — rather than "the
four arms reproduced exactly" — can ship coverage at c = 0.761 on this evidence. What it would still
owe is clause (iv): the second population, the fixture's questions cut to five per node, was never
built, so nothing here shows the constant is a property of the query rather than of this store.

## L2 — the BM25 indexes held by the resident context: shipped

The gap, from [the perf results](2026-09-06-perf-results.md): a resident `serve` holds the graph,
the questions, the vectors, the model and the cross-encoder across answers, and `query::ask` rebuilt
the passage, questions and code indexes from the graph on every question. That rebuild is almost all
of 49 of the lexical arm's 54 ms through the socket, against a 30 ms target. "The number to beat is
49 ms."

### The rule, as pre-registered

> **Rule.** Rule 1 and Rule 2 of the perf plan, verbatim in effect: four dumps and four bench lines
> byte-identical to the baseline on the fixture in both arms; `serve` byte-identical to in-process
> on all 142 cases in all four server × client arm pairings on the copy. Then the clock: socket
> lexical answer after the first, `ask --stale`, base and head binaries in alternating blocks of
> eleven in one sitting on the copy, three blocks each; the change counts only if head's median is
> below base's by more than the spread of base's own block medians. The 30 ms target is reported
> met or missed; it does not decide shipping, the bytes and the spread do.

### Rule 1 — sixteen verdicts, all IDENTICAL

Two rounds of four dumps and four bench lines, against Task 1's dumps and Task 0's bench lines, on
the final binary (sha1 `596192cebb1e97c348f96a89ae9dcc81b13493d9`, the same both rounds —
`$G/t4fix2-rule1.txt`). Condensed here from the file's sixteen individual `IDENTICAL` lines to one
line per round — this block is a summary, not a transcript quote:

```
round 1   dump t1-rec-dense · t1-rec-lexical · t1-dev-dense · t1-dev-lexical      IDENTICAL ×4
          bench rec-dense · rec-lexical · dev-dense · dev-lexical                 IDENTICAL ×4
round 2   the same eight against t4fix2b-*                                        IDENTICAL ×8
```

Rule 1 was re-run in full after each of the two fix rounds below (`$G/t4-rule1.txt`,
`$G/t4fix-rule1.txt`, `$G/t4fix2-rule1.txt`) — sixteen IDENTICAL verdicts each time.

One byte-level finding is worth keeping: `LexicalIndex::attainable` sums an empty iterator when its
index has no documents, and Rust's `Sum for f32` gives that empty sum as `-0.0`. The first Rule 1
run failed on nothing but the sign of that zero in `attainable_code`/`attainable_questions` across
all 82 developer records — invisible to `==` and not to `cmp`. `dump`'s absent-index fallback is
`-0.0` for exactly that reason.

### Fix round 1 — the code index waits for a request that can use it

The first head built all three indexes eagerly. On `$G/bc-a1`, a store with `enrich --code`
questions, that third build is paid by every plain one-shot answer for a list the plain fusion never
seats. Measured with `REPOGRAPH_NO_SERVE=1 <binary> --repo $G/bc-a1 --no-dense ask --stale 'штраф за
отмену записи'`, wall time around the process, alternating blocks of eleven, three each
(`$G/t4fix-codeindex.txt`, commit-to-binary mapping in `task-4-report.md`):

| binary | block medians | median of 33 | spread |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| base (`9ea0281`) | 128.0 · 128.0 · 128.4 | **128.3** | 0.4 |
| head (`6971d68`) | 149.0 · 146.2 · 145.5 | **146.8** | 3.4 |

Head sits 18.5 ms above base (146.8 − 128.3), far past base's 0.4 ms spread. The file's own summary
line prints `delta -18.6 ms`, computed from unrounded medians; the 18.5 ms is the difference of the
two medians as printed, and it is the figure `src/index/lexical.rs` cites. Fixed by making the code

serves a whole run), and a `Context` passes `rerank.is_some()`, with `ensure_code` adding the list
later if a `--rerank` on the same context needs it.

### Verdict

## L4 — a prompt that knows a decision from a behaviour (G14): stopped at its diagnostic

The gap, from D1: the documents' questions regenerated by sonnet read the largest held-out gain of
the 2026-09-05 campaign — 103 → 132 and 109 → 128, both significant — and lost four of nine `rule`
answers in both arms: `ADR-005`, `ADR-031`, `INV-07`, `INV-10`. The candidate cause was that
`enrich` asks one behaviour-shaped prompt of a requirement, an ADR and an invariant alike.

### The gate, as pre-registered

> **Diagnostic first, and it is free.** The sonnet store (`bc-d1`) and the fixture (haiku) both hold
> 13 questions for each of the four lost nodes and the one gained. They are read side by side and
> each question is counted as rule-shaped (may I / is it allowed / what forbids / what was decided
> and why) or behaviour-shaped (where / how / what happens). L4 proceeds only if sonnet's lines
> carry fewer rule-shaped questions than haiku's on at least three of the four lost nodes;
> otherwise the cause is elsewhere, the prompt is not the lever, and G14 is recorded as such.

### The count

Both stores' `questions.json` read question by question, the synonyms line excluded from both
buckets (`$G/g14-diagnostic.md`, tagged inline for auditability):

| node | haiku rule-shaped | sonnet rule-shaped | sonnet below haiku? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| ADR-005 | 4 | 5 | no — reversed |
| ADR-031 | 3 | 2 | **yes** |
| INV-07 | 7 | 9 | no — reversed |
| INV-10 | 8 | 8 | no — tied |
| ADR-003 (gained, not lost — context only) | 6 | 5 | yes, but excluded from the verdict |

1 of 4. The gate requires 3 of 4.

### The second reading

Because a hand count decided a lever, a second reader was asked for the same classification, given
only the two `questions.json` files and neither the first count nor the plan. It wrote its bucket
rules down first and then classified every line (`$G/g14-recount.md`):

| node | A (haiku) rule-shaped | B (sonnet) rule-shaped | B strictly lower? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| ADR-005 | 4 | 5 | no |
| ADR-031 | 6 | 4 | **yes** |
| INV-07 | 8 | 9 | no |
| INV-10 | 10 | 9 | **yes** |
| ADR-003 (gained, not lost — context only) | 7 | 5 | yes, but excluded from the verdict |

2 of 4. The two readings disagree on absolute counts — the second reader's counts run higher on
three of the four gate nodes (ADR-031 6 vs 3, INV-07 8 vs 7, INV-10 10 vs 8) and agree exactly on
the fourth, ADR-005 (4 and 5 in both readings) — and agree on the two things that decide the gate:
`ADR-031` moves the way G14 predicted, and `ADR-005` and `INV-07` move the *wrong* way. Sonnet asks
**more** permission-shaped
questions there, not fewer.

### Verdict

**Stopped.** Both readings miss the pre-registered bar of 3 of 4, so G14's stated cause — the prompt
drifting toward "where do I find this" under a stronger generator — is not what the questions show.
`src/enrich.rs` is untouched, no prompt was written, no `enrich` was run, and **no tokens were
spent** (the budget was ≈ $0.06 on haiku and ≈ $0.32 on sonnet, both estimates from
[the design note](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-0.5.0-gaps-design.md), both unspent).
Neither measurement (a) nor (b) of the design note was taken, so nothing here changes what the README
says about `enrich_command = sonnet` beyond what the campaign already recorded.

What the diagnostic does establish is narrow, and it is worth stating as narrowly as it holds: on
four nodes, under two independent hand classifications, the rule-shaped share of sonnet's questions
is not systematically lower than haiku's. That is not a finding about why those four `rule` answers
were lost — the retrieval side was never looked at — only that the prompt's shape is not the
difference the counts show. Both tables are recorded question by question, so a third reader can
overturn this without regenerating anything.
