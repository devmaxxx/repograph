# G14, read a second time: the loss is register, not shape

**Date:** 2026-09-06, on `main` at `d394273` (0.5.0, with the coverage admission).
**Stores, both read-only:** the pinned fixture `beauty-crm-502e8a6d` (haiku questions) and
`/Users/max/bench/gaps-2026-09-05/bc-d1` (the same graph, documents' questions regenerated by
sonnet — the D1 lever). **Measurement files:** `/Users/max/bench/coverage-2026-09-06/`,
`d1-*.txt`, `d1-dev-lexical.json`, `g14-ranks.txt`, `g14-vocabulary.txt`. No tokens spent; every
command here is `bench` or `dump`.

D1 is the strongest paraphrase lever this project has measured and the one it did not ship:
paraphrase 17/30 and 16/30 against 15/30 and 14/30, held-out 103 → 132 (p = 0.0003) and
109 → 128 (p = 0.032), and one kind lost — the developer suite's `rule` questions fall 5/9 to 2/9
in both arms. G14 named a cause: the enrichment prompt is the same for a requirement, an ADR and
an invariant, so under a stronger generator the questions drift toward "where do I find this"
where the case asks "is this allowed". The 2026-09-05 diagnostic counted the question shapes by
hand and did not support that: sonnet asks *more* permission-shaped questions on two of the four
lost nodes, and two independent readers put the count at 1 and 2 of 4 against a bar of 3.

This is the second reading the register asked for — the retrieval side rather than the prompt.

## Three hypotheses, all dead

### The admission was not it

D1 was measured under the ratio gate. 0.5.0 replaced that gate with the coverage admission, so the
first question was whether the change in what gets seated recovers the loss. It does not.

| arm | D1 under the ratio | D1 under coverage |
|---|---|---|
| recorded, dense | 40/17/12 p90 228 | 40/17/12 p90 227 |
| recorded, lexical | 39/16/12 p90 222 | 39/16/12 p90 227 |
| developer, dense | 37/60, `rule` **2/9** | 38/60, `rule` **2/9** |
| developer, lexical | 36/60, `rule` **2/9** | 37/60, `rule` **2/9** |

Both developer totals rise by one, entirely outside `rule`. Case by case in the lexical arm, the
same four nodes fall and the same one is gained as under the ratio: the fixture hits `ADR-005`,
`ADR-031`, `INV-07`, `INV-10` and `NFR-RPT-1`; D1 hits `ADR-003` and `NFR-RPT-1` and nothing else.
A complete change of the form that decides whether the questions list is seated moved none of the
nine. (`d1-dev-{dense,lexical}.txt` against `bench-dev-{dense,lexical}.txt`.)

That is worth stating plainly because it is the cheapest hypothesis anyone would try next, and it
is wrong: the questions list is admitted in **every** one of the nine cases in both stores.

### Query length was not it

Long queries could have diluted the match. The `rule` cases are the *shortest* kind in the suite —
median 24 words against `long`'s 35 and `multi`'s 31 (`g14-ranks.txt`).

### A generator writing permission questions about everything was not it

If sonnet scattered permission-shaped questions across all 1,996 nodes, the real ADR and INV nodes
would lose their distinctiveness. It does not: store-wide, permission-shaped questions are 9.5% of
haiku's 26,117 and 10.2% of sonnet's 27,394, on 72.4% and 73.2% of nodes. Median questions per node
is 13 in both (`g14-vocabulary.txt`).

## What is actually happening

The expected node does not lose its seat. It loses its **rank inside the questions list**, and it
loses it because its own score falls while the field around it rises.

| case | haiku rank | own | field top | sonnet rank | own | field top |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ADR-005` | 3 | 22.82 | 23.81 | 13 | **14.66** | 23.10 |
| `ADR-031` | 8 | 18.90 | 21.10 | 59 | **16.83** | 28.64 |
| `ADR-031` (2nd case) | 6 | 21.26 | 25.50 | 10 | 19.79 | 28.49 |
| `INV-07` | 1 | 26.01 | 26.01 | 19 | **16.29** | 30.04 |
| `INV-10` | 1 | 24.33 | 24.33 | 8 | **15.14** | 24.56 |
| `INV-14` | 14 | 14.27 | 21.05 | 49 | 13.64 | 25.03 |
| `INV-16` | 30 | 14.88 | 24.54 | 4 | 20.03 | 22.71 |
| `NFR-RPT-1` | 1 | 22.59 | 22.59 | 1 | 25.40 | 25.40 |
| `ADR-003` | 3 | 15.87 | 17.13 | 2 | 21.98 | 24.00 |

The median rank of the expected node in the questions list goes 3 → 10 for `rule` and 1.5 → 1 for
`long`. For `cross`, `multi` and `where` the expected node is not in that list at all in either
store, in any case — those cases expect files and code, which the documents' questions index does
not hold, which is why no admission form was ever going to move `where` off 0/9.

So the four lost answers were not out-argued by better competitors. Their own questions stopped
matching the question being asked.

## Why: the two generators write in different voices

`INV-07` says the loyalty programme belongs to the salon and never to the platform. The developer
asks: «Хотим начислять клиентам общие баллы за записи через наш каталог, одинаковые во всех
салонах — можно вообще завести такую сущность в схеме?»

Haiku writes the node's questions in the asker's voice, and some of them in the first person:
«Копятся ли бонусы, если я хожу в разные салоны?», «Что отвечать гостю про общие бонусные баллы?»,
«**Можно ли завести общую для всех салонов** накопительную систему?» — that last one shares
`можно`, `завести`, `общую`, `всех`, `салонов` with the query almost word for word.

Sonnet writes them in the document's voice, and writes them better: «что не даёт платформе завести
собственные бонусы», «какое ограничение в базе запрещает бонусы платформы», «проверяется ли это
правило при обновлении структуры базы». Precise, system-side, and using the document's own terms —
`платформа`, `ограничение схемы`, `внешний ключ` — where the developer said `салон`, `каталог`,
`сущность`.

Measured across the suite, as the share of the query's own words (longer than three characters)
that appear anywhere in the expected node's questions:

| kind | n | haiku | sonnet | shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rule` | 9 | 0.195 | 0.139 | **−29%** |
| `multi` | 12 | 0.347 | 0.378 | +9% |
| `cross` | 15 | 0.135 | 0.138 | +3% |
| `long` | 15 | 0.186 | 0.245 | **+32%** |

One kind moves away from the query's vocabulary and every other kind moves toward it. That is the
whole result, and it explains the rest of D1's profile at the same time: the held-out questions are
generated from the documents, so they speak the document's register and sonnet's precision helps
them; the paraphrase suite paraphrases documentation, same register; the `rule` cases are the only
ones written in a developer's colloquial permission-asking voice, and they are the only ones that
lose.

## What this means for the lever G14 proposes

A prompt per node kind, asking for "is this allowed / what forbids it" questions on ADR and INV
documents, is aimed at the wrong thing twice. Sonnet already writes that shape — more of it than
haiku on two of the four lost nodes — and shape is not what the retrieval lost. Building it would
most likely produce more precisely worded permission questions in the document's own register,
which is exactly what is already failing.

What the evidence points at instead, in the order it should be tried:

1. **Ask for register explicitly, not kind.** One instruction in the existing prompt — some
   questions in the words a person would use rather than the document's, first person allowed — is
   a smaller change than a prompt per kind and is aimed at the measured cause. Judged under the
   same three-way rule.
2. **Keep both sets.** Haiku's questions and sonnet's are complementary on this evidence: sonnet
   wins `long`, paraphrase and held-out, haiku wins `rule`. The union costs both generators' tokens
   and an index roughly twice the size, and needs no prompt design at all. Worth pricing before
   designing anything.
3. Only then a per-kind prompt, and only if 1 and 2 fail.

## What stays true

`ENRICH_COMMAND` stays haiku, for the reason it always did: the developer suite, not this
diagnostic. `enrich_command` remains per-store configuration, so any store that wants sonnet's
paraphrase and held-out recall can have it in one line of `repograph.toml` at roughly five times
the token cost, and take the `rule` loss knowingly. G14 stays open, with its stated cause and two
further hypotheses now measured and dead, and a named cause in their place.
