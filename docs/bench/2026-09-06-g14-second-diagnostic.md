# G14, read a second time: the loss is register, not shape

**Date:** 2026-09-06, on `main` at `d394273` (0.5.0, with the coverage admission).
**Stores, both read-only:** the pinned fixture `beauty-crm-502e8a6d` and
`/Users/max/bench/gaps-2026-09-05/bc-d1` — the same graph, the documents' questions regenerated by
a second generator (the D1 lever). **Measurement files:** `/Users/max/bench/coverage-2026-09-06/`,
`d1-*.txt`, `d1-dev-lexical.json`, `g14-ranks.txt`, `g14-vocabulary.txt`, `union-*`. No model
tokens were spent anywhere in this document: every command is `bench`, `dump` or the local
embedder.

D1 is the strongest paraphrase lever this project has measured and the one it did not ship:
paraphrase 17/30 and 16/30 against 15/30 and 14/30, held-out 103 → 132 (p = 0.0003) and
109 → 128 (p = 0.032), and one kind lost — the developer suite's `rule` questions fall 5/9 to 2/9
in both arms. G14 named a cause: the enrichment prompt is the same for a requirement, an ADR and
an invariant, so under a stronger generator the questions drift toward "where do I find this"
where the case asks "is this allowed". The 2026-09-05 diagnostic counted the question shapes by
hand and did not support that: the document set carries *more* permission-shaped questions on two of the four
lost nodes, and two independent readers put the count at 1 and 2 of 4 against a bar of 3.

**Naming.** The finding below is about *how* a node's questions are written, not about which model
wrote them, so this document names the two sets by their register: **the colloquial set** — the
fixture's, in the words a person asking would use — and **the document set** — D1's, in the
vocabulary the document itself uses. For provenance, once: the colloquial set came from the
configured default enricher and the document set from a stronger model of the same family. Nothing
in the result depends on either being a particular vendor's model, or on one being stronger.

This is the second reading the register asked for — the retrieval side rather than the prompt.

## Three hypotheses, all dead

### The admission was not it

D1 was measured under the ratio gate. 0.5.0 replaced that gate with the coverage admission, so the
first question was whether the change in what gets seated recovers the loss. It does not.

| arm | D1 under the ratio | D1 under coverage |
|---|---|---|
| recorded, dense | 40/17/12 p90 228 | 40/17/12 p90 227 |
| recorded, lexical | 39/16/12 p90 222 | 39/16/12 p90 227 |
| developer, dense | 37/60, `rule` **2/9** | 38/60, `rule` **2/9** |
| developer, lexical | 36/60, `rule` **2/9** | 37/60, `rule` **2/9** |

Both developer totals rise by one, entirely outside `rule`. Case by case in the lexical arm, the
same four nodes fall and the same one is gained as under the ratio: the fixture hits `ADR-005`,
`ADR-031`, `INV-07`, `INV-10` and `NFR-RPT-1`; D1 hits `ADR-003` and `NFR-RPT-1` and nothing else.
A complete change of the form that decides whether the questions list is seated moved none of the
nine. (`d1-dev-{dense,lexical}.txt` against `bench-dev-{dense,lexical}.txt`.)

That is worth stating plainly because it is the cheapest hypothesis anyone would try next, and it
is wrong: the questions list is admitted in **every** one of the nine cases in both stores.

### Query length was not it

Long queries could have diluted the match. The `rule` cases are the *shortest* kind in the suite —
median 24 words against `long`'s 35 and `multi`'s 31 (`g14-ranks.txt`).

### A generator writing permission questions about everything was not it

If the document set scattered permission-shaped questions across all 1,996 nodes, the real ADR and INV nodes
would lose their distinctiveness. It does not: store-wide, permission-shaped questions are 9.5% of
the colloquial set's 26,117 and 10.2% of the document set's 27,394, on 72.4% and 73.2% of nodes. Median questions per node
is 13 in both (`g14-vocabulary.txt`).

## What is actually happening

The expected node does not lose its seat. It loses its **rank inside the questions list**, and it
loses it because its own score falls while the field around it rises.

| case | colloquial rank | own | field top | document rank | own | field top |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ADR-005` | 3 | 22.82 | 23.81 | 13 | **14.66** | 23.10 |
| `ADR-031` | 8 | 18.90 | 21.10 | 59 | **16.83** | 28.64 |
| `ADR-031` (2nd case) | 6 | 21.26 | 25.50 | 10 | 19.79 | 28.49 |
| `INV-07` | 1 | 26.01 | 26.01 | 19 | **16.29** | 30.04 |
| `INV-10` | 1 | 24.33 | 24.33 | 8 | **15.14** | 24.56 |
| `INV-14` | 14 | 14.27 | 21.05 | 49 | 13.64 | 25.03 |
| `INV-16` | 30 | 14.88 | 24.54 | 4 | 20.03 | 22.71 |
| `NFR-RPT-1` | 1 | 22.59 | 22.59 | 1 | 25.40 | 25.40 |
| `ADR-003` | 3 | 15.87 | 17.13 | 2 | 21.98 | 24.00 |

The median rank of the expected node in the questions list goes 3 → 10 for `rule` and 1.5 → 1 for
`long`. For `cross`, `multi` and `where` the expected node is not in that list at all in either
store, in any case — those cases expect files and code, which the documents' questions index does
not hold, which is why no admission form was ever going to move `where` off 0/9.

So the four lost answers were not out-argued by better competitors. Their own questions stopped
matching the question being asked.

## Why: the two sets are written in different registers

`INV-07` says the loyalty programme belongs to the salon and never to the platform. The developer
asks: «Хотим начислять клиентам общие баллы за записи через наш каталог, одинаковые во всех
салонах — можно вообще завести такую сущность в схеме?»

The colloquial set writes the node's questions in the asker's voice, some of them in the first person:
«Копятся ли бонусы, если я хожу в разные салоны?», «Что отвечать гостю про общие бонусные баллы?»,
«**Можно ли завести общую для всех салонов** накопительную систему?» — that last one shares
`можно`, `завести`, `общую`, `всех`, `салонов` with the query almost word for word.

The document set writes them in the document's voice, and writes them better: «что не даёт платформе завести
собственные бонусы», «какое ограничение в базе запрещает бонусы платформы», «проверяется ли это
правило при обновлении структуры базы». Precise, system-side, and using the document's own terms —
`платформа`, `ограничение схемы`, `внешний ключ` — where the developer said `салон`, `каталог`,
`сущность`.

Measured across the suite, as the share of the query's own words (longer than three characters)
that appear anywhere in the expected node's questions:

| kind | n | colloquial | document | shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rule` | 9 | 0.195 | 0.139 | **−29%** |
| `multi` | 12 | 0.347 | 0.378 | +9% |
| `cross` | 15 | 0.135 | 0.138 | +3% |
| `long` | 15 | 0.186 | 0.245 | **+32%** |

One kind moves away from the query's vocabulary and every other kind moves toward it. That is the
whole result, and it explains the rest of D1's profile at the same time: the held-out questions are
generated from the documents, so they speak the document's register; the paraphrase suite paraphrases documentation, same register; the `rule` cases are the only
ones written in a developer's colloquial permission-asking voice, and they are the only ones that
lose.

## The variable is register

Nothing above is a property of a particular vendor, family or size of model. What was measured is
two sets of questions in two registers and what each does to retrieval; which generator produced
which set is provenance, not mechanism. Two consequences follow, and both matter more than the
model names:

- **This is not "the stronger model is worse."** The document set is better written and wins four
  kinds of five. It loses one because that kind's queries are phrased the way a person speaks while
  its questions are phrased the way a document is written. Any other pairing that produced the same
  two registers should reproduce the same split.
- **Register is a prompt variable, so one generator can supply both.** The prompt decides register
  at least as much as the model does. A single generator asked to write some of a node's questions
  in the asker's words and the rest as now should recover much of what two generators buy, at one
  generator's cost. That is the cheapest thing on the list and the first to try.

Which model runs either stage is `enrich_model` and `rerank_model` — a repository key, a machine
key or an environment variable, never a constant in the source. The evidence says the register the
prompt asks for is what to control; the model is the operator's choice.

## What this means for the lever G14 proposes

A prompt per node kind, asking for "is this allowed / what forbids it" questions on ADR and INV
documents, is aimed at the wrong thing twice. The document set already writes that shape — more of
it than the colloquial set on two of the four lost nodes — and shape is not what the retrieval lost. Building it would
most likely produce more precisely worded permission questions in the document's own register,
which is exactly what is already failing.

What the evidence points at instead, in the order it should be tried:

1. **Ask for register in the existing prompt, one generator.** Some of a node's questions in the
   words a person would use, first person allowed; the rest as now. One instruction, one
   generator's tokens, aimed at the measured cause, and no second pass to reconcile. Judged under
   the same three-way rule.
2. **Two generators, both sets kept.** The two registers are complementary on this evidence. Priced
   below: it beats both single sets on every kind either suite measures, at both generators' tokens
   and an index twice the size. Not pre-registered, so it ships nothing on that reading.
3. Only then a per-kind prompt, and only if 1 and 2 fail.

## What stays true

The default enricher is unchanged, for the reason it always was: the developer suite, not this
diagnostic. `enrich_model` is configuration — per repository, per machine, or per run — so a store
that wants the document register's paraphrase and held-out recall can have it in one line, at
roughly five times the token cost, and take the `rule` loss knowingly. G14 stays open, with its stated cause and two
further hypotheses now measured and dead, and a named cause in their place.

# The union, measured and deliberately not shipped

**This section is exploratory.** No rule was written before these numbers, so nothing here may
ship on them. It is recorded because the effect is large, because it cost no model tokens to see,
and because the next attempt should start from it rather than rediscover it.

If register is the variable, the crudest possible test is to put both registers in one index. The
D1 store was copied, the colloquial set merged into it per node, and the copy's 26,098 new rows embedded by the local `e5-small` — 58.5 s, zero model tokens,
since the token cost of a question is paid when it is *generated*, not when it is indexed. 1,996
nodes, 53,492 questions, a median of 26 a node against 13 and 13. All three stores were then read
by the same binary at `d394273` under the coverage admission (`union-summary.txt`).

## What is wrong with this evidence

**No rule was written first.** Everything above was read after the fact. This project's own history
is the argument for why that matters, and the coverage admission two commits ago is the argument for
what to do instead: write the clauses, commit them, then measure.

**The held-out set favours any store holding the colloquial questions.** It was built from the fixture's
questions, one per node, with the question's own text removed from its node at dump time. Its 12
siblings stay. So a store containing that set has a home advantage the document-set store never
had, and part of the union's held-out margin is that advantage returning rather than a new
gain. The recorded and developer suites are hand-written and carry none of this; they are the
numbers to trust, and they say paraphrase 15 → 20 and `rule` 5 → 7 in the lexical arm.

**The store was merged by a script, not produced by `enrich`.** Nothing in the tool can generate
two sets of questions today, and nothing decides what happens when one generator's pass is stale
and the other's is not.

**The cost is two generators.** A fresh store pays both passes — measured at ≈ $2.50 and ≈ $13.62
for the two used here — a
questions index twice the size, and a minute more embedding. Whether one paraphrase in three and
two `rule` answers are worth ≈ $16 a corpus is a decision, not a measurement.

## What a pre-registered attempt should say

Written before it runs, in the shape the coverage admission used: keyword and code hold their
floors in all four arms with p90 at or under 230; paraphrase is above the better single generator
in both arms; `rule` is at or above the colloquial set's 5/9 in both arms and no other developer kind is below
the better single generator; held-out is not worse in either arm, judged on a set built from a
generator that is not in the store, or from both, so the home advantage is removed. That last
clause is the one this reading cannot supply for itself.
