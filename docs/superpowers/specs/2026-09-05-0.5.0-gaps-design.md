# 0.5.0 — closing the measured gaps: design

The version bump to 0.5.0 is [PR #7](https://github.com/devmaxxx/repograph/pull/7). This note
decides what else 0.5.0 carries: which of the open gaps in
[`next-version-gaps.md`](../../bench/next-version-gaps.md) and which of the perf leftovers in
[`2026-09-06-perf-results.md`](../../bench/2026-09-06-perf-results.md) are worth a lever now, what
each lever is, what rule it is judged by — written here, before any number is read — and what it
costs. The plan that argues from this note is
[`../plans/2026-09-05-0.5.0-gaps.md`](../plans/2026-09-05-0.5.0-gaps.md).

Nothing in this note is implemented. Where a number appears it is a number an earlier document
measured and names its source; no number below is a prediction.

## The four levers

| | lever | raised by | what it costs | what closes it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | a scale-free admission for the generated-questions list, then the same admission tried for the code list with one seat | G12 (and G8, which it subsumes; G13 is what it is judged on) | zero tokens; dumps and a replay script | the rule below, in two stages |
| L2 | the BM25 indexes held by the resident context instead of rebuilt per question | the perf results: "the number to beat is 49 ms" | zero tokens; one sitting of socket timings | Rule 1, Rule 2, and a timing rule |
| L3 | floors for the default embedder, so `bench` on a default store grades something | README: "until it has floors of its own, a green `bench` on a default store says less" | zero tokens; four `bench` runs on the surviving e5-large copy | four measured numbers keyed by model, and `bench` refusing to grade a model it has no floors for |
| L4 | an enrichment prompt that knows a decision from a behaviour | G14: D1's `rule` 5/9 → 2/9 in both arms | under a dollar of tokens: 47 ADR and INV nodes regenerated, twice | the diagnostic first, then the rule below |

The order is the order of the table: L1's replay costs nothing and decides whether `query.rs`
changes at all; L2 touches the same function and goes after it; L3 and L4 touch neither. L4 is
last because it is the only one that spends tokens and the only one whose first step can end it.

### L1 — the admission (G12 → G8, read against G13)

**What is wrong.** `QUESTIONS_GATE` admits the generated-questions BM25 list to the plain fusion
when its best score is at least 0.85 of the passage list's best (`src/query.rs:53`). The two
scores are in two units: each index normalises length against its own mean (30.6 tokens a
passage document, 51.8 a question document) and weights terms by its own vocabulary (10,762
terms against 21,839). A2 proved the ratio moves when a population moves and nothing else does —
`FR-WH-53`'s ratio went 0.80 → 1.03 when code questions entered the questions index, and the
`--no-dense` arm lost a keyword case on a change that added no document to its passage index.
The window on the 82 is (0.802, 0.866], 0.006 of room above. All of that is G8 and G12 as
recorded.

**What changes.** Each list gets a statistic of its own, so the two compare in one unit, and the
admission compares those. Three forms, tried in this order and no other:

1. **coverage** — a list's best score over what the query could attain in that index: the score
   a document of average length holding each query term once would get, which BM25 makes the sum
   of the terms' idf. `LexicalIndex` gains `attainable(query)`. The admission is
   `coverage(Q) / coverage(P) ≥ c`.
2. **peak** — a list's best over its own fifth score (five is the seat count, an existing
   constant, not a new one); a list shorter than five is a peak of its own. The admission is
   `peak(Q) / peak(P) ≥ c`.
3. **z** — the best as a z-score over the list as the plain path sees it, 20 deep. The admission
   is `z(Q) / z(P) ≥ c`.

Each form has one constant `c`, and `c` is not chosen by looking at a suite. It is the crossover
on the 400 held-out questions in the `--no-dense` arm — the arm whose floors the gate exists to
protect — defined as the split of the sorted statistic that puts the most questions on the side
of the list that holds their answer in its top five (the questions list above, the passage list
below), ties to the lower value. That is how 0.85 was set in ADR-001 Amendment 6; this makes the
derivation a procedure rather than a reading. The 82 and the 60 are opened only after `c` is
fixed. The first form whose replay passes the rule is the one implemented; the others are not
tried in the binary.

**Two stages.** Stage A replaces the ratio for the questions list and touches nothing else.
Stage B, only if A passed, admits the code-questions list to the plain fusion under the *same*
form and the *same* `c`, capped at one seat (A4's shape) — no second constant, because a constant
chosen for the code list by looking at the developer suite is how a suite stops measuring
anything (G13). Stage B is what A4 failed under the ratio; the admission is the only thing
different.

**Rule for stage A** — every clause, none re-read after the numbers:

- (i) the recorded suite reproduces the four arms **exactly** on the fixture store:
  `40/40 15/30 12/12` and `39/40 14/30 12/12` enriched, `40/40 9/30 12/12` and `39/40 7/30 12/12`
  raw, p90 ≤ 230 in each;
- (ii) the 400 held-out questions are not significantly worse in either arm against the shipped
  binary's dumps (paired exact McNemar, α = 0.05, `bench/heldout.py compare`);
- (iii) the developer suite (60) is not down in either arm;
- (iv) on a second population of the same corpus — the fixture's questions cut to their first
  five per node, zero tokens, deterministic — the new admission is not significantly worse than
  the ratio on that store's own held-out set in either arm, and reads at least what the ratio
  reads on the 82 there. This is the clause that says "scale-free" rather than "fitted again".

**Rule for stage B** — the three-way rule A2–A4 were read against, on the surviving `bc-a1` store
(the one with code questions): every recorded floor held in both arms, held-out on that store's
document questions not significantly worse in either arm, developer suite not down in either arm
**with `where` above 0/9**. A4 read `where` 2/9 at six held-out questions lost in each arm,
p = 0.031; that is the number to beat and the clause it failed.

**Stop.** A form that fails a clause is recorded with its numbers and the next form is tried;
three failures end L1 — the ratio stays, G12 is recorded as measured, and G13's table is
regenerated under each form so the next attempt starts from evidence. Stage B failing does not
undo stage A.

### L2 — the resident BM25 indexes

**What is wrong.** A resident `serve` holds the graph, the questions, the vectors, the model and
the cross-encoder across answers, and `query::ask` rebuilds the passage, questions and code
indexes from the graph on every question. The perf results bound that rebuild at almost all of
49 of the lexical arm's 54 ms through the socket, against the design's 30 ms target
(`2026-09-06-perf-results.md`, "The lexical arm misses the 30 ms target"). `bench` pays it 82
times and `dump` once per query on top of the three it already builds itself.

**What changes.** `index::lexical::Lexical` — the three indexes built once from a graph and its
questions. `query::ask` takes one by reference instead of building. `ask::Context` builds it
lazily on the first answer, at the point where today's build overlaps the model open, and drops
it when the graph or the questions move (`adopt`). `bench` and `dump` build one and reuse it;
`dump` builds it from its leave-one-out questions, exactly the indexes it already builds for its
own lists. Nothing is written to disk: the indexes are term statistics over every document, a
rebuild is the cheapest correct update, and there is still no lexical state that can go stale
relative to the graph.

**Rule.** Rule 1 and Rule 2 of the perf plan, verbatim in effect: four dumps and four bench lines
byte-identical to the baseline on the fixture in both arms; `serve` byte-identical to in-process
on all 142 cases in all four server × client arm pairings on the copy. Then the clock: socket
lexical answer after the first, `ask --stale`, base and head binaries in alternating blocks of
eleven in one sitting on the copy, three blocks each; the change counts only if head's median is
below base's by more than the spread of base's own block medians. The 30 ms target is reported
met or missed; it does not decide shipping, the bytes and the spread do.

### L3 — floors for the default embedder

**What is wrong.** `passes` in `src/bench.rs` keys the floors on `(enriched, dense)` and the
numbers were measured on `multilingual-e5-small`. The default is now `multilingual-e5-large`
(PR #5). A default store clears the small model's dense floors "with room" — G11 measured
`40/40 22/30 12/12 p90 224` enriched with embeddings — so green there is a floor the store never
met, and the raw dense arm on the large model was never measured at all.

**What changes.** The floors gain a model key. Dense arms read the small model's floors when the
store's rows are the small model's or unnamed, the large model's floors when they are the large
model's, and are **measured and not graded** under any other model — the summary line says
`model=small|large|<name>` and `gated=false` for the last, the way another case file is measured
and not graded today. The lexical arms have no embedder and keep one set of floors. The large
model's numbers are measured, not copied: enriched and raw dense arms on the surviving e5-large
copy of the fixture, each twice, floors set at the measured counts with p90 rounded up to the
next ten — the discipline `passes`'s own comment states. `bench/history/track.py` reads the
floors out of the Rust source and must keep doing so under the new shape.

**Rule.** The small model's four arms print the same lines as before on the fixture (Rule 1's
bench half); the large copy's two dense arms print the same counts on both runs; the history
gains rows for both.

### L4 — the enrichment prompt knows a decision from a behaviour (G14)

**What is wrong.** D1 regenerated the documents' questions with sonnet and read the largest
held-out gain of the campaign — 103 → 132 and 109 → 128, both arms significant — and lost four of
nine `rule` answers in both arms: `ADR-005`, `ADR-031`, `INV-07`, `INV-10`. `enrich` asks one
behaviour-shaped prompt of a requirement, an ADR and an invariant alike; the `rule` questions ask
"is this allowed, what forbids it".

**Diagnostic first, and it is free.** The sonnet store (`bc-d1`) and the fixture (haiku) both
hold 13 questions for each of the four lost nodes and the one gained. They are read side by side
and each question is counted as rule-shaped (may I / is it allowed / what forbids / what was
decided and why) or behaviour-shaped (where / how / what happens). L4 proceeds only if sonnet's
lines carry fewer rule-shaped questions than haiku's on at least three of the four lost nodes;
otherwise the cause is elsewhere, the prompt is not the lever, and G14 is recorded as such.

**What changes.** A prompt for `Adr` and `Invariant` nodes that asks for the questions a person
about to do the forbidden thing would ask, batched apart from the documents' prompt as the code
prompt already is. The passage hash does not change with the prompt, so a store regenerates only
the entries that are removed first — the 27 ADR and 20 INV nodes.

**Two measurements, one shipping decision.** (a) The new prompt on haiku, the shipped generator,
on a copy of the fixture with the 47 entries regenerated: judged by the three-way rule against the
fixture — every floor in all four arms, held-out not significantly worse in either arm, developer
suite not down in either arm — with `rule` at 5/9 or better in both arms. This is what can ship
as the default prompt. (b) The new prompt on sonnet on a copy of `bc-d1` with the same 47
regenerated: judged against D1's own numbers — `rule` back to 5/9 or better in both arms with
paraphrase at 17/30 and 16/30 or better, held-out not significantly worse than D1's dumps taken
on this binary, every floor held. (b) does not change the default generator, which is a cost
decision and not this note's; it decides what the README says about `enrich_command = sonnet`.

**Stop.** The diagnostic can end it; (a) failing ends the prompt change; (b) failing is recorded
and changes a sentence, not code.

## Explicitly not in 0.5.0

- **G1's symbol half and G6.** 57 of the 61 unnamed symbols are Kotlin declarations, and the
  gate for a new language is a case file in its own corpus. Both are the 0.6.0 extractor work
  and nothing in 0.5.0 can move them.
- **G2.** Every cheaper lever is measured and rejected, `--rerank-local` last; e5-large as a
  store option already reads paraphrase 22/30 where this gap was raised at 16/30. What remains is
  the model, which G11 shipped.
- **G10's seating by agreement between lists.** Untried, and the one shape left there — but A4
  priced any seat given to another list at six held-out questions in each arm, none gained. L1's
  stage B is the same question asked with a per-question confidence signal instead of a turn;
  if it fails, agreement seating is the next shape and needs a design of its own.
- **Persisting the BM25 indexes to disk.** L2 keeps them resident, which is where the 49 ms was
  measured. A one-shot `ask` pays one build per process today and would pay one after L2; a disk
  form buys the one-shot lexical arm at the price of a stale-state story the graph does not have.
- **`mmap` for the vectors.** `vectors loaded` is +15.5 ms in every dense arm, under 3 %.

## The rules, in one place

- **Three-way rule for a retrieval change** (from
  [`2026-09-05-dev-cases-results.md`](../../bench/2026-09-05-dev-cases-results.md)): (i) the
  recorded suite holds every floor in all four arms with p90 ≤ 230 — enriched `40/14/12` with
  embeddings and `39/11/12` with `--no-dense`, raw `40/9/12` and `39/7/12`; (ii) the held-out set
  is not significantly worse in either arm (paired exact McNemar, α = 0.05); (iii) the developer
  suite is not down in either arm. L1 stage A tightens (i) to "reproduced exactly" and adds (iv).
- **Order of operations** (`bench/heldout.py`'s header): held-out dumps on the old binary and
  the new, `compare`, decide, then `bench` as the smoke test. Never the 82 first.
- **Rule 1** (perf): four dumps and four bench lines byte-identical to the baseline, on the
  fixture, both arms. **Rule 2**: `serve` byte-identical to in-process, 142 cases, four arm
  pairings, on the copy.
- **Rule 3**: a lever that fails its rule is written down as "не получилось" with its numbers,
  and no constant is tuned after a suite has been read.
- **Floors** are measured counts; only p90 is rounded, up to the next ten.

## Budget

Tokens: L4 only — 47 nodes on haiku (≈ $0.06 at D1's per-node figure of $2.50 over 1,996) and 47
on sonnet (≈ $0.32 at $13.62 over 1,996); both estimates, both stated as such. Wall clock: L2's
timing sitting is minutes; L3's four bench runs on the large model are ~0.8 s a question; L1's
replay is seconds per form and its live confirmation is the usual eight dumps and eight bench
lines per stage. Nothing re-embeds a store.
